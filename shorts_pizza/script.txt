She called 911 to order a pizza — and that pizza saved her mother.

Ohio, November. A 911 dispatcher picks up. A woman says: "I'd like to order a pizza."

Most dispatchers would hang up.

Tim Teneyck didn't. "Ma'am, you know you've called 911?" "Yes."

And then he understood. She couldn't talk. Someone was right there with her.

So he asked only yes-or-no questions. "Are you in danger?" "Yes."

He sent police — and told them to turn off their sirens before they got there.

Officers arrested a man for domestic violence.

"Pizza" isn't an official 911 code. He just listened.

And if you ever can't talk, in many places you can text 911 instead.

Because sometimes "I'd like a pizza" means "Help me."
