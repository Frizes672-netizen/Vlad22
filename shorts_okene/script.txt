A diver reached for a dead man's hand — and the hand grabbed back.

May 2013. A tugboat flips in a storm and sinks thirty meters to the bottom of the Atlantic.

Twelve men on board. The cook, Harrison, was in the toilet.

In total darkness, he feels his way to a tiny pocket of air.

Cold water up to his chest. No food. No light. And the air slowly turning to poison.

One day. Two days. Almost three.

Then — a light.

Divers came to recover the bodies. One of them sees a hand and grabs it. The hand squeezes back.

But the most dangerous part was still ahead. After sixty hours under pressure, going up too fast would kill him.

So they sealed him in a chamber for sixty more hours.

He walked out alive. The only one of twelve.
