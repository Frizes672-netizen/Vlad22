They stole $71 million in Bitcoin.

But six years later, those same Bitcoin were worth over $4.5 billion.

They thought they had gotten away with the perfect crime.

Until the FBI knocked on their door.

And what investigators found next was absolutely shocking.

In 2016, someone hacked a cryptocurrency exchange and stole nearly 120,000 Bitcoin.

The exchange was called Bitfinex.

After the hack, the stolen Bitcoin disappeared into the digital world. The criminals moved the money through complicated transactions, hoping to make it impossible to trace.

But there was one problem.

Bitcoin transactions leave a public trail.

And investigators kept following it.

Eventually, they identified a man named Ilya Lichtenstein and his wife, Heather Morgan.

In February 2022, federal agents arrested them.

Then came the shocking part.

Investigators discovered a file containing private keys connected to cryptocurrency wallets holding a huge portion of the stolen Bitcoin.

The government seized approximately 94,000 Bitcoin.

At the time, they were worth around $3.6 billion.

It became one of the largest financial seizures in the history of the U.S. Department of Justice.

But how did investigators connect the money to the suspects?

The answer wasn't a dramatic chase or a secret informant.

It was digital evidence.

Transactions that seemed almost impossible to untangle were connected through years of investigation. And a file recovered from Lichtenstein's cloud storage helped authorities access the stolen funds.

In 2023, both Lichtenstein and Morgan pleaded guilty to charges related to money laundering.

Lichtenstein later admitted that he carried out the Bitfinex hack.

Think about that.

When the Bitcoin were stolen, they were worth around $71 million. But as Bitcoin's value increased, the case turned into a multibillion-dollar story.

The criminals tried to move the money.

They tried to hide their tracks.

But the blockchain kept recording every transaction.

And here's the unexpected twist:

The same technology that helped make Bitcoin valuable also helped investigators track down the stolen fortune.

Because on the internet, money can move incredibly fast.

But the digital footprints it leaves behind can last for years.
